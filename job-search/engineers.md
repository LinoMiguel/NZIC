      <h2>Can engineers work in New Zealand?</h2>
      <p>New Zealand has consistent shortages in several engineering disciplines, including:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Civil and structural engineering (infrastructure, roading, water)</li>
        <li>Mechanical and process engineering (food, energy, manufacturing)</li>
        <li>Electrical and electronics engineering</li>
        <li>Aeronautical and aerospace engineering</li>
        <li>Environmental engineering</li>
        <li>GIS and surveying</li>
        <li>Project management (engineering context)</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Many of these are on the <a href="/visa/the-green-list">Green List</a>, which can fast-track your pathway to residence.</p>

      <h2>How to find engineering roles</h2>
      <p>The majority of engineering roles in New Zealand are never publicly advertised — they are filled through networks, referrals, and direct outreach. As an overseas applicant, applying solely to job boards is rarely effective.</p>
      <p>NZIC's Job Seeker Programme, delivered by Lino Miguel, focuses on:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Building a credible professional profile that NZ employers respond to</li>
        <li>Identifying and approaching the right employers in the hidden market</li>
        <li>Preparing for NZ-style interviews, which combine technical and cultural fit assessment</li>
        <li>Coordinating your job search timeline with your visa pathway</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>Engineering NZ membership</h2>
      <p>Engineering NZ (ENZ) is New Zealand's professional engineering body. Having your qualifications assessed and your membership confirmed by ENZ significantly strengthens both your visa application and your standing with NZ employers. Your NZIC adviser will advise on when and how to initiate this.</p>

      <h2>Your immigration pathway</h2>
      <p>Depending on your engineering discipline and years of experience, your likely pathway is either:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Green List Tier 1 or 2</strong> — if your occupation is listed, this may be the fastest route</li>
        <li><strong>Skilled Migrant Category</strong> — via Expression of Interest with a job offer</li>
        <li><strong>AEWV then residence</strong> — work visa first, then apply for residence after two or more years</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>Long-term settlement for engineers</h2>
      <p>New Zealand is a good destination for engineers looking for high quality of life alongside a technically stimulating career. The country has significant infrastructure investment underway, and engineers with the right credentials and language skills are welcomed. NZIC's Fusion Service combines the job search and visa pathways into a single coordinated process.</p>
