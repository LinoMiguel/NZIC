      <h2>Health roles on the Green List</h2>
      <p>Many health occupations are on New Zealand's <a href="/visa/the-green-list">Green List</a> — meaning fast-track residence for workers who meet the criteria. Tier 1 occupations (straight to residence) include many specialist medical roles. Tier 2 occupations (residence after 2 years on a work visa) cover a wider range of nursing and allied health roles.</p>

      <h2>Registration requirements</h2>
      <p>All regulated health professions in New Zealand require registration with the relevant authority before you can practise:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Doctors:</strong> Medical Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Nurses:</strong> Nursing Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Dentists:</strong> Dental Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Pharmacists:</strong> Pharmacy Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Physiotherapists:</strong> Physiotherapy Board of New Zealand</li>
        <li>And many others — check the relevant council's website</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Registration takes time and must typically be initiated before or alongside the visa application. Your NZIC adviser will coordinate the timing.</p>

      <h2>English requirements for health professionals</h2>
      <p>All health registration bodies require evidence of English language ability. Most use OET (Occupational English Test) or IELTS Academic. The minimum scores vary by profession — your adviser will confirm what applies to you.</p>

      <h2>Job market overview</h2>
      <p>DHBs (now Health New Zealand) and private hospitals are actively recruiting internationally. Some roles require a trial period, supervision, or a specific qualification equivalency. NZIC's job search consultants have experience placing health professionals and can advise on the realistic job market in your specialty.</p>
