      <h2>Health roles on the Green List</h2>
      <p>Many health occupations are on New Zealand's <a href="/visa/the-green-list">Green List</a> — meaning fast-track residence for workers who meet the criteria. Tier 1 occupations (straight to residence) include many specialist medical roles. Tier 2 occupations (residence after 2 years on a work visa) cover a wider range of nursing and allied health roles.</p>

      <h2>MCNZ registration pathways</h2>
      <p>All doctors must be registered with the Medical Council of New Zealand (MCNZ) before they can practise. There are several registration scopes:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>General scope:</strong> for GPs and specialists who meet MCNZ's standard requirements</li>
        <li><strong>Provisional scope:</strong> for doctors who need to complete additional supervised practice</li>
        <li><strong>Vocational scope:</strong> for recognised specialists in specific fields</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The registration process typically takes several months and should be initiated well in advance of your planned arrival. Dr. Moeller can advise on what to prepare and when.</p>

      <h2>NZ hospital culture</h2>
      <p>New Zealand's public hospital system (run by Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora) values collaborative teamwork, clinical autonomy, and direct communication. International doctors sometimes find the flat hierarchy different from their home systems. Dr. Moeller's programme includes preparation for this cultural shift.</p>

      <h2>PHO/GP systems</h2>
      <p>New Zealand's primary care is delivered through GP practices affiliated with Primary Health Organisations (PHOs). International GPs can enter the NZ primary care system either as employed GPs or as practice owners. Dr. Moeller has first-hand experience of this transition and advises on the realistic options for your situation.</p>

      <h2>Rural vs. metropolitan practice</h2>
      <p>Rural NZ has significant GP shortages and often offers faster registration pathways, more competitive packages, and excellent quality of life. Metropolitan areas (Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch) are more competitive but offer more specialised practice opportunities. NZIC can advise on what fits your clinical preferences and visa pathway.</p>

      <h2>Working with Māori and Pacific communities</h2>
      <p>New Zealand's public health system has specific goals around reducing health inequities for Māori and Pacific peoples. International health professionals are encouraged to develop cultural competence in this area. Dr. Moeller includes an introduction to these frameworks in the Pre-Arrival Accelerator programme.</p>

      <h2>Registration requirements for other health professions</h2>
      <p>All regulated health professions in New Zealand require registration with the relevant authority before you can practise:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Doctors:</strong> Medical Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Nurses:</strong> Nursing Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Dentists:</strong> Dental Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Pharmacists:</strong> Pharmacy Council of New Zealand</li>
        <li><strong>Physiotherapists:</strong> Physiotherapy Board of New Zealand</li>
        <li>And many others — check the relevant council's website</li>
      </ul>
      <p>Registration takes time and must typically be initiated before or alongside the visa application. Your NZIC adviser will coordinate the timing.</p>

      <h2>English requirements for health professionals</h2>
      <p>All health registration bodies require evidence of English language ability. Most use OET (Occupational English Test) or IELTS Academic. The minimum scores vary by profession — your adviser will confirm what applies to you.</p>
