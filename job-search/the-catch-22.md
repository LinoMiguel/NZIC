      <blockquote>
        To get a skilled migrant visa, you typically need a job offer from a New Zealand employer.<br>
        To get a job offer, NZ employers typically want a candidate with NZ work rights or NZ work experience.<br>
        To get work rights, you need a visa.<br>
        And to get a visa, you need a job offer.
      </blockquote>

      <h2>Why the Catch-22 exists</h2>
      <p>New Zealand employers take very few risks when hiring. They want to meet candidates before making an offer, they prefer someone who can start quickly, and they are cautious about the complexity of sponsoring a visa. From an employer's perspective, hiring a local candidate with work rights is simpler than hiring someone from overseas.</p>

      <p>At the same time, Immigration New Zealand wants to see evidence that an employer genuinely needs an overseas worker — which typically means completing a Job Check and demonstrating that no suitable NZ candidate is available.</p>

      <h2>How migrants get stuck</h2>
      <p>The typical failure pattern:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Applicant applies for advertised roles from overseas, with no NZ address, no NZ experience, and no work rights</li>
        <li>Applications go unanswered or receive polite rejections</li>
        <li>Applicant concludes NZ employers do not want overseas workers</li>
        <li>Applicant gives up, or comes to NZ on a visitor visa, runs out of money, and returns home</li>
      </ul>

      <h2>How to break the cycle</h2>
      <p>Breaking the Catch-22 requires a different approach on both the visa side and the job side simultaneously:</p>
