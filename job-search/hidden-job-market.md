      <h2>What is the hidden job market?</h2>
      <p>Employers fill the majority of roles through internal referrals, direct approaches to known candidates, and professional networks — before they ever post a job ad. When they do post ads, the pool of well-networked local candidates often means the role is effectively pre-filled before overseas applicants even see it.</p>

      <h2>Why it matters for migrants</h2>
      <p>If you are searching from overseas and relying exclusively on job boards, you are competing for a small fraction of available roles, against a large pool of local applicants who have advantages you do not have: work rights, local references, and personal relationships with hiring managers.</p>

      <h2>How to access the hidden market</h2>
      <ol>
        <li><strong>Build your professional network before you arrive.</strong> LinkedIn is your primary tool. Connect with NZ professionals in your field, follow NZ companies, and engage with their content.</li>
        <li><strong>Contact companies directly.</strong> Write to hiring managers and HR managers in companies where you would like to work, introducing yourself and asking whether opportunities exist. This direct-approach strategy is culturally accepted in NZ.</li>
        <li><strong>Use professional associations.</strong> Bodies like Engineering NZ, the NZNO (nursing), or relevant trade associations often have job boards and connections that are not visible to the general public.</li>
        <li><strong>Get an introduction.</strong> A warm introduction from someone who knows both you and the employer is worth more than any CV. NZIC's consultants can make introductions in some cases.</li>
        <li><strong>Visit NZ.</strong> Nothing replaces meeting people face to face. If you can afford a job-hunting trip, it dramatically increases your chances.</li>
      </ol>

      <h2>How NZIC helps</h2>
      <p>NZIC's Job Search Consultants have an established network across NZ employers in many sectors. They know which companies are open to hiring migrants, which prefer to see a visa first, and which can be persuaded with the right approach. They also coach you on the CV format, cover letter style, and interview technique that work in New Zealand.</p>
